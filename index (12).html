01About

The hardest part of hiring isn't finding people — it's knowing which roles to open, in what order, and why. That's the work I do best: turning a growth plan into a hiring plan a leadership team can actually run against.

At Dream11, I've been part of scaling the team from 560 people to over 1,200 — leading hiring across Tech, ML, Product, Design, and Corporate. That's meant closing leadership roles, building campus pipelines at IIT and ISB, and standing up the company's first US hiring program with Columbia University.

Before Dream11, five years at Gupshup through its shift to conversational AI — where I learned what it takes to rebuild a talent pipeline while a company is still deciding what it wants to be. The thread across all of it: making talent acquisition a function leadership plans around, not waits on.

How I think about hiring

Hiring is the most compounding decision a company makes. Every person you bring in changes how the room thinks — what gets built, what gets challenged, who joins next. You're never just filling a role; you're choosing the company you'll become a year from now. So I've stopped asking who's the most qualified, and started asking who will shape what comes next.

Leadership hiring

Executive Search · Closing

The hires where the cost of getting it wrong is highest. Across 3.5 years I've personally closed 12 leadership roles at Dream11 — owning everything from the search and the calibration with CXOs to the compensation conversation and the close.

Why I love this work

Most people think recruiting is about finding people. I think it's about recognising potential before it's obvious — and being the reason someone takes the leap that changes their career.

There's a specific kind of satisfaction in it. You spend weeks understanding a problem a team can't solve on its own, you find the one person who can, and you watch them walk in and change what the company is capable of. Years later they're running the function. You had a small, quiet hand in that.

I've never seen TA as a support role waiting for requisitions. Done well, it's one of the highest-leverage seats in the building — because every other decision a company makes depends on who's in the room to make it. That's why I've stayed in it for thirteen years, and why I keep building tools to do it better.
